Never Split the Difference - Key Sales Techniques

TACTICAL EMPATHY
The foundation of all negotiation. Understanding the feelings and mindset of another in the moment and hearing what is behind those feelings so you increase your influence in all the moments that follow.

MIRRORING
Simply repeating the last three words (or the critical one to three words) of what someone has just said. This encourages them to keep talking and reveals more information.
Example: Customer: "The price seems really high for what we're getting."
Response: "For what you're getting?"

LABELING
Spotting the other person's feelings and turning them into words. Use phrases like "It seems like..." or "It sounds like..." or "It looks like..."
Example: "It sounds like you're concerned about the implementation timeline."

CALIBRATED QUESTIONS
Open-ended questions that begin with "How" or "What" that give the other side the illusion of control while you're gathering crucial information.
Example: "What's the biggest challenge you face?" or "How can we solve this problem together?"

THE ACCUSATION AUDIT
List every terrible thing your counterpart could say about you at the beginning of the negotiation. This disarms them and takes the sting out of potential objections.
Example: "You probably think we're just another software company trying to sell you something you don't need..."

NO-ORIENTED QUESTIONS
People are more comfortable saying "no" than "yes." Frame questions to get a "no" that actually means "yes."
Example: "Would it be a ridiculous idea if we looked at how this could save you time?"

THE 7-38-55 RULE
Only 7% of a message is based on words, 38% comes from tone of voice, and 55% from body language and face. Focus on HOW things are said, not just what is said.

DEADLINES
Deadlines are often arbitrary and almost never trigger the consequences we think. Don't be pushed by false urgency.

BLACK SWANS
Hidden pieces of information that, if uncovered, would change everything. There are always at least three Black Swans in every negotiation.

THE ACKERMAN MODEL
A systematic bargaining approach:
1. Set your target price (your goal)
2. Set your first offer at 65% of target
3. Calculate three raises of decreasing increments (85%, 95%, 100%)
4. Use lots of empathy and different ways of saying "No" before increasing offer
5. On final number, use precise, non-round numbers
6. On final number, throw in a non-monetary item to show you're at your limit
