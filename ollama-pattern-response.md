## PATTERN RULE: Empathy Questions

**When to Use:**
Empathy questions should be used when the prospect's statement indicates they are defending a current solution or status quo, even if the language is neutral. This often signals underlying dissatisfaction or unspoken concerns that need exploration.

**Distinguished From:**
Unlike neutral statements which do not indicate strong opinions or emotions, defensive statements reveal hidden pain points or desires that can be uncovered through deeper questioning to understand their true needs and concerns.

**Instruction:**
"Identify when the prospect defends a current solution as satisfactory but reveals an underlying concern by using language like 'it works' or 'we're used to it.' Use empathy questions to explore why this is important to them, revealing hidden pain points."

**Context Reinforcement:**
If the conversation history includes repeated references to sticking with existing methods despite challenges, it signals a need for deeper inquiry into their underlying motivations and concerns.

**Example Recognition:**
"If prospect defends current method by saying 'it works' then use Empathy Questions because they are likely hiding dissatisfaction or unmet needs."

## PATTERN RULE: Labeling

**When to Use:**
Labeling is appropriate when the prospect's statement reflects strong emotions or experiences of failure, indicating a need for validation and understanding. It helps diffuse tension by acknowledging their emotional state.

**Distinguished From:**
Unlike Take Away which amplifies objections through acknowledgment, Labeling diffuses negative energy by validating feelings rather than addressing practical concerns directly.

**Instruction:**
"When the prospect expresses strong emotions or past failures with phrases like 'we're tired of' or 'burned,' recognize this as a need for emotional validation and use labeling to acknowledge their experience."

**Context Reinforcement:**
If previous statements hint at repeated letdowns from vendors or unsuccessful implementations, it signals a pattern of unresolved negative emotions that require acknowledgment.

**Example Recognition:**
"If prospect expresses frustration with past failures by saying 'every solution has fallen short' then use Labeling because they need emotional validation to build trust."

## PATTERN RULE: Calibrated Questions

**When to Use:**
Calibrated questions are effective when the prospect shows willingness to move forward but also expresses a concern that can be reframed as an opportunity for further discussion. This signals readiness for deeper engagement.

**Distinguished From:**
Unlike Mirroring, which simply repeats back statements without action, Calibrated Questions identify specific concerns and reframe them into opportunities or questions that lead towards commitment.

**Instruction:**
"When the prospect shows interest but also expresses a concern like 'the timing feels off' or 'I need executive approval,' recognize this as an opportunity to reframe their concern positively and proceed with calibrated questioning."

**Context Reinforcement:**
If prior conversation indicates positive engagement mixed with hesitation, it signals the need for careful calibration of concerns into more constructive dialogues.

**Example Recognition:**
"If prospect expresses willingness but mentions 'we're stretched thin,' then use Calibrated Questions because this reveals a solvable concern that can lead to further commitment."

## PATTERN RULE: Buy-In

**When to Use:**
Buy-In is applicable when the prospect actively seeks additional information or asks follow-up questions, indicating readiness for more detailed engagement. This shows genuine interest and willingness to proceed.

**Distinguished From:**
Unlike Dynamic Silence which waits for the next move after positive statements, Buy-In recognizes active requests as signs of commitment and engages with proactive responses.

**Instruction:**
"When the prospect actively seeks information like 'can you tell me more about,' or asks specific questions that show engagement, recognize this as a buy-in opportunity."

**Context Reinforcement:**
If previous exchanges include clear indications of interest followed by queries for further details, it reinforces the need to capitalize on these moments with deeper discussion.

**Example Recognition:**
"If prospect requests 'more information' then use Buy-In because they are actively seeking engagement and commitment opportunities."

## PATTERN RULE: Black Swan

**When to Use:**
Black Swan recognition is crucial when the prospect expresses vague concerns or objections that do not seem directly related to immediate issues but hint at deeper organizational challenges.

**Distinguished From:**
Unlike Summarizing which organizes information, recognizing a Black Swan involves identifying hidden political issues or complex stakeholder dynamics behind seemingly neutral statements.

**Instruction:**
"When the prospect makes vague statements like 'I need to ensure all stakeholders are aligned' while everything seems fine on paper, recognize this as signaling deeper organizational challenges."

**Context Reinforcement:**
If previous conversations have hinted at internal politics or decision-making processes that go beyond surface-level issues, it signals a need for deeper investigation into underlying concerns.

**Example Recognition:**
"If prospect mentions 'stakeholder alignment' with no immediate objections then use Black Swan because this hides deeper political challenges."

---

These rules aim to capture the conversation dynamics and emotional states rather than specific keywords, ensuring that the AI can adapt to various phrasings and contexts.
